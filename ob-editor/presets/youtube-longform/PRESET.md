# Preset: OB long-form YouTube (talking-head teaching video, Dan Go style)

Load this first for any OB long-form edit. These are the locked values OB approved on C4352 (seed oils) and C4353B (the breakfast), 2026-10-16/17.
Idea from Jason Cooperson's Claude video editor course (OB sent it 2026-10-17): lock a dialed-in style into a preset so the next edit gets it right the first time instead of being re-derived.
Rules behind these values: memory `feedback_ob_youtube_edit_style`, `feedback_shot_list_before_render` (HARD RULE: breakdown approved before any full render).

## Pipeline (in order; lock each stage before the next)
0. **The brief first** (OB 2026-10-18; full text at the top of the editor skill):
   - Evidence: his retention curves lined up against past `events_resolved.json`, plus the reference's "Most replayed" graph and pacing.
   - Promise: title and thumbnail locked, so the first 15 s pay them off. (Measured 2026-10-22: saying the promise in concrete words has a small edge at 1:00; paying it off by 0:20 or 0:40, or staying on the title, showed none. See The intro, rules 2 and 5.)
   - Story edit from the raw takes: cut slack and repeats, move the payoff up, re-hooks at known dips. Show him a one-page structure before the breakdown.
   - Clearance ledger `WORK/qc/clearance.csv`.
   - Step 5 after posting: read the curve at day 3 for the first rows and at day 7 for the read that counts, against the Browse band in The intro, and write the lesson here.
1. **Intake.** Camera file + external mic (MOTIV / MainOutput WAV). `sync_av.py CAMERA WAV --audio-only master_synced.wav`. The camera audio is never in the edit (room echo).
2. **Transcribe.** `whisper-cli` medium.en with word times (`-ml 1 -sow -oj`), and chunked base.en per speech chunk for verbatim restarts (medium smooths disfluencies).
3. **Rough cut.** `cutlist.py`: thresh -45 dB (20 ms RMS), `--min-pause 0.6 --keep-before 0.12 --keep-after 0.12 --soft-db -50` (OB 2026-10-18: "make the cuts a little bit faster so its less deadspace": about 260 ms between sentences; the old 0.22 s pads gave 460 ms). `--soft-db` follows quiet word endings ("-tes", a final "s", a "t" release after its closure) and keeps short fragments like "by" next to speech, so the tight pads don't clip words. Outtakes (restarts, flubs, misstatements he corrects himself) go in `--remove` ranges, each edge on the quietest 5 ms between words. Self-corrections count as outtakes: keep the corrected version. **Never cut a claim** (OB 2026-10-18): outtakes only. Keep the cut in a per-video script (`bin/c4365_cut.py` is the pattern) so it can be re-run.
4. **Cut QC.** Run both before graphics, then lock the cut:
   - `react_qc.py build|compare`: word-level diff of the edit against the raw.
   - `cut_audit.py`: every shot edge needs ≥ 60 ms of silence to the first/last word. Every join needs a 140-650 ms pause; mid-sentence joins can be shorter.
   - Check after any audio_shift_ms is applied, because sync nudges eat the padding.
5. **Sync check.** GCC-PHAT of the mic against the camera scratch audio, per shot (`qc/seg_gcc.json` method). The recorder drops buffers, giving a ±50 ms sawtooth. Fix with per-shot `audio_shift_ms` (target: camera hears him ~6 ms after the hand mic). `audio_head_mute_ms` / `audio_tail_mute_ms` mute a sliver at a shot edge.
6. **Graphics plan.** Write `events_spec.json` with anchors from his words (generate it from a `make_spec.py` in WORK; it keeps head_clear's tag slots on re-runs). `resolve_events.py`. Coverage target: 80-90% of the runtime (OB 2026-10-17, "more graphics for retention"), counting his lines on his face. Full-frame cards are NOT 80-90%: OB 10-21 wants his face back between overlays (see "His face between overlays" below; C4367 had full-frame cards 75% of the time and his face 25%, and he called it "overlay to overlay to overlay").
   - Every spoken claim gets something on screen: the study's real number where one exists (PubMed or USDA, loaded first), his own words where none does. Risky lines (sponsor, guarantee, medical advice) get no graphic and go to OB as a flag.
   - Flow: back-to-back panel cards chain; a day-of-eating run carries the `day` tracker; chapter card at each topic change. Check face-only gaps over 8 s with the resolved timeline.
   - Before the breakdown, render `WORK/roughcut.py` (540p, graded, synced mic, frame-exact, no graphics) so OB can judge the flow of the cut itself.
   - Face first 5 s.
   - No face-only stretch over ~8 s, except the intro and the CTA.
   - No graphics on dubious health claims (e.g. the cortisol/hormones lines).
   - **No text-only panels** (OB on C4365, 2026-10-19: "you just put text... not even a photo of them"). Every food, object or number gets a visual, nothing on a panel holds still for more than about 0.5 s, and frame one of every card is finished. Full rules: the editor skill's `references/motion-library.md`.
   - **Optional effects menu (§G of that file, 2026-10-28):** 20 code-built effects OB sent ("don't need to use them all the time just good to have"). In long-form, use them as section changes and emphasis beats (zoom portal into a new chapter, magazine cover on the thesis, digital zoom on a label, headline storm with real sourced headlines), one at a time, then back to the locked look. Built on first use, breakdown first.
   - **No flicker** (OB on the C4367 v7 preview, 2026-10-20: "too many overlays like showing the clip and me talking just looked glitchy"): no cover under ~1.5 s between two shots of his face, no face sliver under ~1.5 s between two covers, no card-face-same-card break under ~2 s. When a look-down forces a short cover, run the cover over the whole line or hold the card before it instead. Scan the painted timeline (the same painter as `flash_check.py`) for both patterns before any render: `bin/flicker_check.py` does it, with the 9-frame sliver check, as part of `precheck.sh`.
   - **Motion upgrades on C4367 v8:** chapter cards `cards.py` chapter v4 (giant outlined number drifting behind, words rising out of a mask, a sweep in the chapter colour across the title, the map as a track where the progress line travels to this part and the node pulses, upcoming parts blurred); `explainer_cards.dose` v2 (columns as heights in 1 g slices with counters, zone bands lighting, symptom chips on his words, real bar photos dropping into "my bars a day" with free-fall and a squash); `photo_full anim="sunburn"` (a figure drawn in code, skin colour stops per beat via a CSS variable). Research behind them: the session's motion spec (WIRED's blurred upcoming levels, Rosling's box-by-box, Haroz et al. 2015 on unit pictographs).
   - **Every named product or flavor shows its own package** (OB on the C4367 breakdown, 2026-10-20: "adding a photo of the bars themselves with the flavors to match would help... for the calories"). Math lines take a 4th field (a 2.65:1 photo tile beside the line), `side_img` puts one tile beside a whole card, versus items take a photo. Cut the tiles from footage already cleared for the video (news frames, product shots) before asking to download, check each label reads, and re-run `story/fit_cards.py` after adding images (the fill-the-frame scale was measured without them and pushes content off the edge).
   - **His lines go on his face, not on a slide** (OB on C4367 v6, 2026-10-20: "show me more than these boring ass text overlay slides... showing my face more and just putting text as the overlay on those shots anyways, just the text ones, i love the animations and slides that have movement"). A line of his is a `facequote` (cards.py: the lines land on his words beside his face, the key phrase underlined, big like the self-quotes) or a caption `plate`; no full-screen text card. Where his eyes are down inside the line, the look-down goes under moving footage (a short `insert` or a muted clip) or under the animated card before it held longer (`extend`), and his face starts the moment his eyes are on the lens: scan the face span in the gaze rows (pupil_y >= 0.45, open < 0.13 or no face = down; on the lens he sits about 0.30-0.36), then confirm on close-up strips. The tracker's look-down runs end early: on C4367 12 of 20 face spans still opened on eyes down or shut. `bin/lookaway_check.py` runs the same scan over every visible stretch of the timeline, not only the face spans.
   - **His face between overlays, and longer clips** (OB 2026-10-21, after watching the published C4367: "Show me on camera more often when I'm talking, you went from overlay to overlay to overlay and sometimes I felt I could've been shown in between a bit"; "the TikToks and the news could've been shown longer"):
     - After every 1-2 overlays, come back to his face for 2 s or more while he's on the lens. No more than 3 full-frame overlays in a row; a montage of up to 4 chained clips counts once.
     - His face on screen 40% or more of the runtime (C4367: 25%). His lines go on his face, not on cards.
     - Clips with their own sound (TikToks, news) play 4-6 s or more, so the line finishes (C4367 median 3.4 s). Muted B-roll inserts run 3 s or more (C4367 median 2.1 s; 53 of 58 were under 3 s). Fewer, longer overlays.
     - Where a look-down would force a chain of covers, cut the look-down out in a pause instead.
     - `bin/pace_check.py` (in `precheck.sh`) fails on all of these: `--min-face 40 --max-chain 3 --min-beat 2 --min-clip-median 4 --min-insert-median 3`. These are starting points set from his feedback, not his numbers; tune them with his next notes. On C4367 it reports face 25%, 18 runs over 3 (the longest 16 in a row), clips 3.4 s and inserts 2.1 s.
7. **Place clear of his face.** `OB_SRC=... head_clear.py WORK --write` writes the tag slots and checks every overlay against his face, sampled every 0.4 s with Apple Vision. Framing changes per video, so run it every time.
8. **Second pass, graphic by graphic** (Cooperson: this pass is what separates a good edit from "AI slop"). Render mocks, tile them and look at every one: placement, overlap with his face or cap, text widows, readability, timing against his words. Check animations frame by frame (`frames_at.js`), including t = 0.
9. **Breakdown → OB's OK.** `breakdown_meta.json` + `build_breakdown.py` + `html2pdf.js`. Send the PDF and also write the decisions in chat.
10. **Render.** First `bin/precheck.sh WORK` must pass (section "Gates and tools", before any preview or final render). Detached:

    ```
    HYPERFRAMES_RENDER_DETACHED=1 OB_SRC=... OB_MASTER=... OB_VBR=40 nohup perl -MPOSIX -e 'POSIX::setsid(); exec @ARGV' caffeinate -i python3 -I bin/assemble.py WORK OUT --keep
    ```

    Only one 4K render runs at a time on the 8 GB Mac; `render_when_free.sh` queues. Edits re-render only what changed (content-keyed cache).
11. **QC.** `render_review.py WORK OUT.mp4 --src CAMERA.MP4 --phone`: frame count vs the cut list, black/frozen frames, loudness, lip sync per shot, frame-by-frame sheets (a frame every 2 s with the graphic ID) and phone parts under 28 MB.
    Then `final_deliver.py WORK OUT.mp4` (it makes no phone copies by default since 10-29; long-form never goes to a phone, the AirDrop folder or iCloud, OB 10-23 and 10-29: he uploads the T7 master from the Mac). It runs the QC gate with the edit's
    full-screen spans (`assemble.is_cover`: a chapter card is not text on his face) and `overlay_touch.py`: every graphic over his A-roll,
    4 times a second, the render minus the bare A-roll against his face box. Text or a box on his face FAILs and blocks (OB 10-25).
    Quote cards are fitted to the widest his face reaches over the whole shot, not the still: `face_clear.py WORK --ids ... --write`
    (kept in story/face_clear.json; build.sh re-applies it). C4395: 22 of 39 quote cards came within 80 px of his face on lean-ins.
12. **Music + deliver.** See Audio. Then the description with chapters, study DOIs and credits, packaging via the `/youtube` skill, and a phone preview (≤ 30 MB parts).

## The intro (his own curves: 132 long-form videos, measured 2026-10-22)
- **His face opens every video (OB 2026-10-30, C4402: "my face is alwasy in the opener"; ledger M35, gate check `opener`):** frame 1 is his talking head, and nothing full-screen (stock, his own b-roll, a clip, a full card) covers him before 3 s. Hook words go ON his face as caption plates (C4395's "15 THINGS / BEFORE I STARTED LIFTING"; C4402 now "99.9% OF PEOPLE / ARE TRAINING WRONG / IN THE GYM"), never as a footage reel. A notes glance inside the hook gets his own footage (C4402: C2310 at the laptop), never stock strangers. Pass 3 of C4402 broke it: reels_v2 turned the H01 caption plate into a stock reel, then a stock glance cover, so his face first showed at 0:06 (`C4402-edit/story/opener_face.py` fixes it after reels_v2). C4395 (5.4 s) and C4398 (15 s) already opened on him.
- **No roadmap, no repeats (OB 2026-10-23, C4372):** "Here's the plan" / a list of questions / "first the timeline, then the real reason" is over-explaining ("Johnny never does that"). The open is: hook, promise, the wild clips, one honest caveat, a short credential with client proof, then straight into the story.
Source: vault 90-References/youtube-demand/_YT-INTRO-RETENTION-2026-10-22.md, ledger C361 to C372.
1. The leak is 0:00-0:20, so the retention work goes there. On all 124 readable curves the biggest drop starts before 0:30 (median 0:09, about 20 points), 58% of everyone who leaves is gone by 1:00, and from 1:30 to the halfway point he is typical for his length. After 2:00 the top cliff is a median 2.3 points and the slide 1.8 points a minute, so pace cuts after 2:00 can only move a small part of the loss.
2. No opening type is proven, so don't force one. Cold claim, "here are N things", title read, question and story (the types with 10+ videos) land at 0.46 to 0.50 relative at 1:00, and with Browse share and length held, clip first and direct address are no different (122 openings, blind-checked). Pick the opening that fits the video. Don't block a cut for its opening type, and don't cut a short credentials line or "welcome back" to fix the curve: neither showed a measurable cost (a credentials line: +0.8 points lost in the next 10 s against videos without one, n 36).
3. Judge every opening against its Browse band, at day 7 (Analytics runs about 3 days behind). Read relative retention at 0:15, 0:30 and 1:00 and compare with the band median: under 40% Browse 0.43 / 0.48 / 0.55; 40-60% 0.43 / 0.46 / 0.52; 60-80% 0.38 / 0.41 / 0.46; 80%+ 0.34 / 0.35 / 0.41. Above the band at 1:00 means the opening did its job. Don't use "lose under 25% by 0:30": 110 of 124 miss it, and the ones that pass are long videos that do no better.
4. One opening change per upload, logged. In the brief, write the opening type, the second the title's subject is named, the second of the first payoff and the change being tested; at day 7 add the Browse share and relative retention at 0:15, 0:30 and 1:00. Lock a change as a rule only with 10+ uploads per side in the same Browse band and a gap of 0.08+ at 1:00. Smaller gaps are this channel's noise at that n: the spread after Browse is 0.09.
5. Say what the video gives the viewer, in concrete terms, inside the first minute, and inside 0:20 where the video allows (his median is 0:14). "Let me explain why", "these four things" and "by the end of this video you'll" count; a topic label ("today we're talking about X") does not. It is the one opening feature with a measured edge, and a small one: +0.04 relative at 1:00, about 2 points more still watching (85 vs 37 openings, timed blind, Browse share held; interval +0.00 to +0.08). The edge shows below 70% Browse, not above. When the first payoff lands (by 0:20 or by 0:40) made no difference.

## Learned on C4367 (46-min read-through, 2026-10-19)
- **"Cut that, Claude":** OB says it on camera after a flub. Drop the call and the try before it, and keep the redo. A cutlist island can hold both, so split it on the quietest point of the pause between them (`story/gaps.py` in the C4367 work folder).
- **Read-then-say takes:** removing the pauses removes his reading time, but he often keeps talking with his eyes down. Scan the raw take with `bin/gaze_raw.py` and calibrate on that take (head pitch while talking vs while reading in the long gaps). Then every eyes-down run inside a kept shot needs a full-frame visual. A side panel doesn't hide it, because his face is still on screen.
- **Sync over long takes:** `bin/lag_curve.py` gives a dense GCC curve. Fit a line per piece between the recorder's dropout steps, and take each shot's shift from that model. With his head turned, a shot's own GCC median can lock onto a room reflection (+6.5 ms, +23 ms). Verify on the delivered file: cut the camera audio the same way and expect a flat +6 ms.
- **The T7 is exFAT through fskit.** One sequential read runs at 130-330 MB/s, but 3-6 parallel ffmpeg readers drop to about 11 MB/s. Copy the source to `OB-ARCHIVE/OB-EDITS-WORKING/` (APFS SSD) before any multi-job render.
- **The gaze rule misses lid-drop glances** (eyes drop, head barely tilts: pitch < 0.38, pupils not low). Runs of 3+ samples with openness < 0.2 caught 15 more on C4367, but a frame strip showed only 7 were reading; the rest were long blinks, squints and laughs. Confirm by eye, then log them in `story/gaze_manual.json`, which `story/coverage.py` checks along with the tracker's runs.
- **Card layout (explainer_cards.py, 2026-10-20 critic pass):** checklists are a centred block; text-only versus cards centre vertically; square product photos use `layout="split"` (a full-frame crop cut the David wordmark); dose labels are top-aligned and pin labels clamp inside the frame; a horizontal clip with a pull quote shrinks left so the quote fits. Read every mock at full size: the 4x3 contact-sheet labels hide card titles.
- **Breakdown with clip holds:** `build_breakdown.py` shows a hold's clip text from `story/clip_heard.json` (each finished clip's audio run through whisper; also a check that no clip starts or ends mid-word) and needs `stills/shot_###.jpg` (`story/shot_stills.py`). Export the PDF at 1320 px wide: Letter cuts off the 1100 px table.

## Learned on C4367 v3/v4 (fill the frame, score, 2026-10-20)
- **No flat black, ever** (OB: "so much black space", "switch up the background... make it prettier"). `bin/stage.py` dresses every full-frame card: its own clip or photo blurred and moving, his frame blurred warm, or a chapter colour wash, rotated so neighbours differ, plus a push-in, grain and slow camera beats in any 3 s+ quiet stretch. A card body that paints its own full-frame background hides the stage (the disclaimer did): start every card body with `<div class="bg"></div>`, and grep for `inset:0;background:#` when adding a type. A test range only passes its own events, so `stage.assign` reads `WORK/events_resolved.json` for chapter colours and the rotation (a test once showed amber where the full render shows crimson).
- **Text cards must move** (OB: "boring slides that stay there for 5 seconds doing nothing"). `quote` cards land line by line on his words (`at={"l1": "phrase", "l2": ...}`; line 0 at the start), sized by the longest line (`min(10300 / chars, 1560 / (1.1 x lines), 460)` px), and the lines said so far stay centred as the block grows. Re-split a long line into the phrases he says. Clip cards with a pull quote play centred with the blur on both sides until the quote lands, then slide over; the quote is centred in the free side and sized by its length (`EPG` 740 px).
- **Score cue starts:** pick each cue's start from a sustained section of the track (scan its 0.5 s levels). A sparse section (Fragments of Bangkok at 35 s: median -28.7 dB, 7 dB swings) left the title card almost silent, 37 dB under his voice. Before any render, build the bed on the whole voice (`score.build`) and measure it per chapter against a **stereo** copy of the voice (a mono voice reads 3 LU quieter): every chapter about 22-24 LU under, its 3 s loudness spread under about 2.5 dB, the silences silent, the title swell the only peak.
- **Preview copies keep the master's audio** (`-c:a copy`). Re-encoding the AAC at 192k pushed the true peak from -1.4 to +0.5 dBFS.

## Editing pickups into a finished cut (C4367 + C4368, 2026-10-20)
OB: "edit them together now... add it in where it needs to be... the whole color grade, sound sync, mute so there is no echo... deadspace... be as exact as you got in the first batch"; then "cut to me... overlay text from me on screen talking... like Johnny Harris".
- **One source.** Join the camera files (`ffmpeg -f concat -c copy`, `-map 0:v -map 0:a`) and the synced masters (atrim the first to its exact sample count, then concat) so every tool reads one SRC and one MASTER. Old source times stay the same; new time t sits at t + (old frames x 1001/30000). Check frame hashes at both ends of both files before trusting it.
- **Transcribe each new island alone** (whisper base + medium, padded 0.5 s): the long-context DTW pass invented words ("You'll be fine" for "Mostly fine", "Goals" heard into a cut). Pick takes from that, and record the choice per script line in `c4368/story/keepers.py` (island, or (island, from, to, (head ms, tail ms))).
- **Cut at measured points, not word times.** Find gaps with a 10 ms trace, then go to 5 ms RMS + zero-crossing rate where words touch: the C4367 proof-line cut first landed in the /d/ closure of "day" and dropped its vowel; "goals" ended in a /z/ 300 ms after its DTW time. Re-hear every trimmed piece alone and in the edit.
- **Story order**: the v3 cut script lists the whole story as desk islands, pickup lines and clip holds; the flow pass, sync, holds, frame grid and framing run unchanged on top. `resolve_events.py` maps a source time outside every shot to the next shot in SOURCE order and an end anchor on a shot's last frame to that shot's out point (a reordered story broke the old "next shot in edit order").
- **Sync per source.** The new recorder's residual lag is a sawtooth (about -46 ppm with 2-6 ms upward steps); in a bright room a reflection ~6.5 ms late wins some windows, so use the lower envelope: per shot, the median of its windows within 1.5 ms of their 25th percentile.
- **Level per source.** Measure speech level (70th percentile of 100 ms RMS) per source in the kept shots: C4368 came in 5 dB under the desk; +5 dB on its half of the playback master matched it (noise floors both under -95 dB).
- **Grade per source.** `assemble.py` reads a segment's `pre_vf` (filters before the house LUT). The bright room: `hue=h=4:s=0.75` (face hue 11 -> 15.5, the desk's 15.6) and, after the clouds came in, `eq=gamma` up to 1.13 set from the shot's frame-mean luma: the face only dropped 9% while the room dropped 31%, so match the face, not the frame.
- **Look-downs in pickups.** He reads the prompt off to the side, so add yaw (< -0.5) to the first batch's pitch rule; then eye-check every 0.33 s+ run on 3-frame strips: 11 of 19 were real glances (covered with full cards), 5 were blinks and laughs (left on camera).
- **His face, with his words on it** (the caption plate from the motion library §E): `type: "plate"`, 2-4 words a beat, condensed caps on a black plate, one yellow word; plus pills and self-quotes. Plates start on their shot's first frame (`shift: 0.08`). Face share went from 19% to 23-25% with every look-down still covered.

### Precheck gate (before any preview or final render)
`bin/precheck.sh WORK` runs five read-only checks in this order and stops at the first failure. Exit 1 means the check found problems, 2 means it could not read its inputs; `--keep-going` runs all five and prints a table. Run it after every `resolve_events.py` and right before every preview or render: fix, rerun, then render.

| Check | Fails when | Usual fix |
|---|---|---|
| `coverage_check.py` | a recorded eyes-down run (`story/gaze_shots.json`, `gaze_manual.json`) is not under a full-frame card; a face-only stretch over 8 s outside the intro and the CTA; a cover in the first 5 s. Warns under 80% graphics | a card, an insert, or a face break's card |
| `flicker_check.py` | a cover under 1.3 s between face shots; a face stretch under 1.3 s between covers; a face break inside one card under 2 s; any run under 9 frames; a clip-hold frame with no cover. 1.3-1.5 s is listed as "near", not counted | run the cover over the whole line or hold the card before it; allow-list a real gag |
| `lookaway_check.py` | 2+ neighbouring gaze samples with eyes down (pupil_y >= 0.45, eyes shut under 0.13, or no face) while his face is on screen | an insert or a clip over the look-down, the card before it held longer, or cut it |
| `fit_check.py` | the stored `story/fit.json` pushes a card more than 40 px outside the safe area, or leaves 15% or more of the scale-up undone (measures the card mocks in one headless Chrome, about 15 s) | `fit_check.py WORK --write`, then look at the cards |
| `pace_check.py` | under 12 visual changes a minute, or a median shot over 3.5 s; his face under 40% of the runtime; more than 3 full-frame overlays in a row with no 2 s face beat; clips with sound at a median under 4 s, or inserts under 3 s (OB 10-21) | inserts, face breaks, punch-ins; for the face rules: lines on his face, longer clips, look-downs cut out in pauses |

- **Allow-list** for an intentional gag, by event id: `OB_FLICKER_ALLOW=ID,ID`, or `story/flicker_allow.json` (`{"allow": {"ID": "why"}}`), or `"allow_flicker": true` on the event. C4367 has one: `V3_1B_rat`, the 0.70 s rat gag.

Only samples where his face shows count. A 2-sample blink (eyes shut, so pupil_y means nothing) and a flat 0.45-0.48 with the eyes wide open are listed but not counted (`--show-filtered`; `--strict` counts them). Pickups where he reads off to the side: `OB_LOOKAWAY_ARGS="--yaw -0.5"`. A run eye-checked as expression goes in `story/gaze_exempt.json`.

### Real document pages (`doc_page.py`)
`python3 -I bin/doc_page.py PDF PAGE hf/assets/docs/NAME.png hf/assets/docs/NAME.json "phrase" ["phrase" ...]` renders the page (220 dpi) and finds each phrase (whole words; case, punctuation and line breaks ignored; the first match, so use a phrase long enough to be unique) in the PDF's text layer (`pdftotext -bbox`) first and, when that crashes or lacks the phrase, by Apple Vision OCR (`bin/ocrfind`, built from `bin/ocrfind.swift`, rebuilt here if missing). ### Proxy copy of the camera file (`make_proxy.sh`)
Once per camera file, never while a 4K render runs (it decodes the whole file): `bin/make_proxy.sh /path/CAMERA.MP4` writes `CAMERA.proxy.mp4` next to it: 960x540, H.264, a key frame every 15 frames, no B-frames, the same frames and timestamps as the source (frame N and time T mean the same in both), no audio, a few hundred MB an hour of talking head. A still comes out of it in about 50 ms against about 480 ms from the 4K file. It never overwrites and does not rename a copy whose frame count differs.
- **Reads the proxy:** stills for mocks and breakdown sheets (`still.sh`, `stills.py`, `story/shot_stills.py`) and frame or eye strips. Set `OB_SRC=CAMERA.proxy.mp4`. Their crop numbers are in 4K pixels, so multiply by 0.25 (the punch-in `crop=3200:1800:320:120` becomes `crop=800:450:80:30`) or put `scale=3840:2160,` in front of the old crop. Anything over 960 px (the 1920x1080 mock stills) is an upscale: fine for a mock, not for a thumbnail.
- **Gaze scans: not worth it.** On a 20 s window of C4367 the proxy scan (`gaze_raw.py --crop 400:325:275:88 --width 800`) agreed with the 4K rows on 93% of samples (pupil_y off by 0.03 on average), found all 6 runs and added 2 more, and ran only about 1.5x faster, because the Vision pass costs as much as the decode. Scan the 4K file once per raw take.
- **Reads the 4K source:** every render (`assemble.py`), `thumb_frames.py` and hero stills, `head_clear.py` (it crops in 4K pixels and reports 4K coordinates), `match_grade.py`, `sync_av.py`, `vmaf_check.py`, and any QC of the final picture.

### B-roll edge check (`broll_edges.py`)
`python3 -I bin/broll_edges.py WORK [--all] [--json OUT]` is report only. For every window in `story/broll.json` and `story/clips.json` (the finished cut from `clips_prepped.json`) it looks 0.4 s either side of the in point and the out point in the SOURCE file for a hard cut or a dissolve, by frame differences on a 160x90 gray copy (a dissolve is told from a pan or a zoom by its histogram change and by its middle frame being the average of its ends), and counts the frames of the other shot that the window would keep. INSIDE: one or more frames of the other shot are in the window, so the clip opens or ends on a flash. EDGE: the cut is within half a frame of the in or out point. It prints the in/out to use instead, half a frame before the cut (an input `-ss` keeps the frames at or after it). About a second per edge on 1080p clips at `-threads 2`. Run it after `prep_broll.py` / `prep_clips.py` and before the breakdown. On C4367 it checked 105 windows: 9 INSIDE edges (RR1 ends on 0.40 s of a split screen, XPO opens on 0.35 s of the ABC clip, CBS3 and BRH open on 0.2 s, F2 ends on one frame of the next shot; RR1 and XPO confirmed on frames) and 6 EDGE. ## Gotchas already paid for
- GSAP on SVG: never scale to exactly 0; use 0.001 and pin `svgOrigin` on every tween. Give `tl.set` at time 0 `immediateRender:true`, otherwise frame 0 shows the end state. Fixed in `bowl_anim.py`.
- Inline CSS transforms on SVG elements override GSAP's transform attribute: set start states with GSAP.
- HyperFrames cancels with `render_cancelled_parent_exited` unless `HYPERFRAMES_RENDER_DETACHED=1`.
- pgrep watchers match themselves: use the bracket pattern `[b]in/assemble.py`.
- **Cut lists must use the render's frame grid.** assemble.py lays each shot as fr(src_out) - fr(src_in) frames; a cut list that sums continuous durations drifts from that grid (C4365: 3 frames over 55 shots; C4367: 7 frames over 323), so word-timed cards land late and clip cards leave hold frames uncovered (assemble exits). `c4367_cut.py` now snaps; generic `cutlist.py` still writes continuous times, so snap it the same way before a long edit.
- Fork sessions share `~/ob-editor` and can work in the same project folder. Check file mtimes and coordinate before editing someone's cut list.
- A mock only proves what has landed by its shot time. C4365's cycle mock was shot 0.06 s before its title appeared, so the breakdown hid a title running under the middle-row steps; the final's frame sheets caught it at 9:05. `mock_shots.js` now waits for the cycle title. For any new card type, shoot the mock after its last element lands, and still read every frame sheet of the final.
- **Pace pass (C4367 v2.1, 2026-10-20; OB: "edit like Johnny", "a lot of clips and overlays").** Measure first, like the reference: `story/pace.py` (visual changes a minute, median shot, face share; runs under 0.3 s fold into the shot before, they are flicker, not cuts). Under his rules (every look-down covered, face first, no PiP) the levers are: (1) **face breaks**: `story/face_breaks.py` finds 1.3 s+ stretches inside long cards with his eyes up, clear of every card beat; confirm each on 8 close-up face crops and reject any where he is reading a number or item the card shows; `--apply` writes `holes` into events_resolved.json after every resolve; (2) **inlays**: events with `inlay: true` and a `parent` (`rel` seconds for a clip hold, source anchors for a card) sit on top while the parent keeps playing (a clip's voice runs straight through: the J-cut); `assemble.py` paints frame by frame and resumes the parent at its offset; (3) **punch-ins**: `story/punch_ins.py` toggles framing once in any 5 s+ stretch of him alone (video only, voice untouched; writes `punch_at` into cutlist.json, so rerun after the cut script).
- Inlay footage: every piece must be at least as long as its slot (the insert card does not loop), and check its first and last frames: downloads hide dissolves and 1-3 frame shot changes at the edges (find cuts with frame differences, not scene detection, which missed dissolves). Archive and VHS footage reads as authentic at 320-640 px under the film look.
- **Slivers:** an event edge within 0.3 s of a shot boundary left 1-4 frames of the neighbouring shot (38 in C4367). `resolve_events.py` now puts such edges on the cut, and back-to-back events touch (the old 0.05 s gap blinked). `story/flash_check.py` must report 0 runs under 9 frames before a render (`bin/flicker_check.py` includes that check).
- Anchors match the transcript's spelling: numbers are digits ("12 hours", not "twelve hours"); the fuzzy matcher scored "six hours" as a hit and the 12-hours row landed early. Doc highlights use a background sweep with box-decoration-break so a phrase that wraps still highlights; source tags on news footage sit top left (the network's own lower third is at the bottom).
- **Speech flow (C4367 10-20; OB: "make it sound fluid, not breaks and pauses and sound like im reading a script").** When he reads a phrase at a time, the cut leaves ~0.26 s of air at every join, most of them mid-sentence: that even phrase-gap-phrase rhythm is what sounds read. Measure with `story/pauses.py` (assign words by START time; whisper end times run seconds into the silence and mislabel the join). Fix in the cut script (`c4367_cut.py` step 3b): the air after the last sound (soft edge -50 dB) is set by the punctuation of the word before it: mid-sentence 0.09 s, comma 0.16 s, sentence end 0.24 s (only ever shortened); pauses of 0.22 s+ inside a shot are cut the same way, removing only frames under -50 dB; SPLIT/MUTE edges and joins before a clip stay put. Verify: every removed piece is near-silence (loudest frame), and each word the transcript-timing test flags is still heard in a transcription of the new edit (the timing test cries wolf on long whisper word ends).
- **Clip placement:** a clip that cuts in mid-sentence ("CBS reported on [clip] it.") is a flow bug. List every clip's last/first words (start-time assignment) after each cut; move the clip to the nearest sentence end with `FORCE_SPLIT` (split an island at a quiet point) + `HOLD_AT` (clip after a given piece). When the word runs straight into the next with no gap, move the clip to a nearby sentence end instead of splitting speech.
- After a re-cut, anything authored in edit time must be pinned to source first: `make_spec.py` maps through a frozen copy of the authoring cut; face-break verdicts live in `story/face_break_verdicts.json` by source time (the finder only reads it; confirmed breaks are clamped to the eye-checked span); look-down runs map by source time, not shot index. (`cards.py --mock` writes `ID.mock.html`, separate from the render's `ID.html`, so mocks can be refreshed during a render.)
