## 0. Beat sheet (read this first)

### 0.1 Five signature moves you can put on any footage

1. **The evidence sits in the room with him.** He talks beside a big paper chart on a board, a scorecard card, a list, a product line-up: not in front of a blank wall and not behind a full-screen slide. In 1955 (the closest model for a timeline film) he is composited beside the graphic for **50% of the runtime**, his face is on screen **67%** of the time, and the most-replayed moment of the whole video (11.7x the median) is him beside the chart board while the line answers an objection (15:13). Then the same chart goes full frame for **2.6 s** and he is back.
2. **One red mark at a time, on the word.** A highlight bar, an underline, a hand-drawn circle or a stamp lands when he says the thing, draws in **0.4 to 0.8 s**, then holds while the page keeps pushing in slowly (about 1.5% a second). Red is "look here" on scans, photos and charts; yellow is the highlighter on printed clippings, PDFs and web pages.
3. **Everything is paper or film.** Charts on cream paper or a framed board, documents as aged scans with grain and a vignette, archive inside a rounded projector gate on black, maps in watercolour or on parchment. Nothing sits as a small flat element on flat black.
4. **One device keeps score and comes back.** A fixed card is built once and refilled every chapter: the 1955 scorecard (income, housing, equality, the American dream, a red decade stamp), Tap Water's A/B/C product cards and its claim card with a verdict stamp, the single anchor chart. The viewer learns it once; after that only the change is news.
5. **Cut on the breath, a beat before the word; keep the motion inside the shot.** Cuts fall in speech pauses about twice as often as chance and land in the 100 ms before the next word starts (0.2). Transitions are hard cuts; the movement comes from pushes, highlight sweeps and line draws inside each shot. Fast up front, slower and more face-led toward the verdict.

### 0.2 Cuts per second, and what the cuts are anchored to

- **Rate.** 0.14 to 0.38 hard cuts a second in the six videos (8.5 to 22.9 a minute; median 19, a cut every 3.2 s on average). Median shot 1.7 to 4.2 s (median of the six: 2.0 s). Got Milk (2023) was faster: 31.6 a minute, median shot 1.4 s. The 2025 videos are slower than 2021-2023 Johnny because more of the change happens inside composited shots (1955: 8.5 cuts a minute while the graphic beside him keeps changing).
- **Breath.** 11 to 30% of cuts fall inside a speech pause (his level 10 dB down for 120 ms or more), against 8 to 13% of random moments: **1.2 to 2.4x chance** (median 1.9x). Strongest in 1955 (2.4x), Got Milk (2.3x), USA 1.0 (2.2x), Tap Water (2.0x).
- **Word.** With YouTube's word timings (Groceries, Tap Water) the cut lands **in the 100 ms before a word starts** 37 to 45% of the time against 30% for random moments, and in the 100 ms after a word starts only 20 to 24% of the time against 29 to 31%. The next word starts within 250 ms after the cut 60 to 63% of the time (random 44 to 46%). He cuts just before a word, never into one.
- **Beat.** A music onset sits within 40 ms of the cut 29 to 38% of the time against 22 to 29% at random: a weak 1.2 to 1.4x lift, mostly because new cues start on section turns. The talking body is **not cut to the beat**; only the short montages are.
- **In one line: word and breath first, music second.** Put each cut in the gap, about 2 to 4 frames before the next word.

## 2. Text and captions

- **No running captions on his narration.** None of the seven videos burns in subtitles for Johnny. Text on screen is always a designed object (a label, a title, a quote, a stamp). Third-party clips keep their own captions and chyrons.
- **Type changes per video.** Each video gets its own art direction, title style included (one video's titles were modelled on the fuzzy 1980s-Soviet-TV titles of HBO's Chernobyl, per his interview in section 11). Seen here: typed chapter cards on a real typewriter (JFK), engraved small-caps serif on parchment (USA 1.0), a heavy serif over moving water with a chromatic glow (Tap Water), cream serif titles on cream paper (Groceries), retro signage and red label tags (1955). One system per video, held for the whole video.
- **Recurring roles:**
  - Chart titles: bold condensed caps with a smaller explanatory line under it; series named at the line's end in the line's colour; a small source line bottom-left.
  - Lower thirds: name in heavy condensed white caps, role in small caps under it, **no box**, set beside the face at mid-height (JFK) or bottom-left with a small coloured square (1955); on for the length of the shot (1.7 s at JFK 4:04).
  - Handwritten notes: white handwriting with a drawn arrow pointing at a person (McDonald's 0:57).
  - His own lines as pull quotes: heavy white condensed caps in quotes with his name under it, on a dedicated shot of his face, **0.8 s** (Got Milk 12:43); other one-word versions, one with a red X through it.
  - Keyword emphasis: one word recoloured red inside a stream of text (McDonald's 3:07).
  - Verdicts: a small coloured rating tag above, a dark red box under the quoted claim (Tap Water 21:03).
  - A sources-in-the-description tag with a document icon bottom-left on his frame (1955 2:13).
  - Credits: role in a cream serif, name in white bold caps, on black paper with a rule and two dots.
- **Where text sits:** beside his face, never across it; scorecards and lists top-left (the 1955 card about 45% wide when full, a thumbnail when parked; Tap Water's list about 25% wide); stamps centred under the claim; sources bottom-left and small.

## 3. Structure (as the edit shows it)

- **Chapters:** 5 to 11 YouTube chapters per video. On-screen chapter cards in some videos, 0.6 to 3.2 s (title and date inserts: median 2.0 s, n=7): the typed chapter card (JFK 5:03, 3.2 s), an engraved chapter word (USA 1.0 20:55, about 0.9 s, then a blur into the next shot), two chapter words over moving water (Tap Water, 2.8 s and 2.3 s, his face on both sides).
- **Dates and places get their own card,** before the event: the Dallas place-and-date card (JFK 3:39, 4.8 s), "6000 BC" with a dry aside (Groceries 1:22, 1.6 s).
- **The fixed device returns at every stop** (scorecard, A/B/C cards, anchor chart) and is the main thing that changes between chapters.
- **The reveal is shown, not told:** prop in hand, then the same prop top-down; document in hand, then the scan with the line lit.
- **Order of evidence:** a third-party analysis of his videos calls it visual anchors first, context after (section 11).

## 4. Pace

- **Face beats** (his face on screen, jump cuts inside it): median 5.4 s across the six, p90 8 to 74 s. A face beat holds 1.5 to 2 presenter shots; presenter shots run 2.3 to 3.6 s (1955: 7.3 s, long takes beside the board).
- **Insert runs** between face beats: median 2 shots (4.6 to 10.6 s), p90 6 to 15 shots; 21 to 52% of runs are longer than 3 shots. History sequences run long without him: 78 s (Groceries 6:49), 119 s (the JFK open), 130 s (USA 1.0 16:41).
- **Slivers:** 10 to 30% of his face beats are under 2 s and sit between two inserts (USA 1.0 28%, McDonald's 30%). OB's rule forbids this; here Johnny's habit is the thing to avoid.
- **Clips with their own sound:** most are short stabs (median 2.5 s), but the clip that carries the story plays long: Groceries' Fox clip 10.4 s; JFK's 1963 news-conference and interview clips 9 to 20 s; Groceries' news reports and expert interviews 5 to 11 s. (Measured as stretches where a voice that is not his is heard over a non-presenter shot.)
- **Muted inserts under his narration:** median 1.7 s; 44 to 94% under 3 s.
- **Insert mix** (288 inserts sampled evenly across the six, labelled by eye; median length): archive film and photos 27% (1.8 s), documents and articles 13% (2.0 s), illustrated or animated scenes 11% (3.1 s), stock and location b-roll 10% (1.6 s), charts 7% (**4.8 s, the longest**), social posts 6% (1.5 s), maps 5% (3.5 s), his own b-roll 5% (1.4 s), interviews 4% (4.1 s), product shots 4% (1.6 s), title and date cards 2% (2.0 s), news 2%. ## 6. How he is shot

- **A-cam:** frontal mid shot; his face is 18 to 30% of the frame height in 68 to 78% of presenter time (1955, McDonald's, USA 1.0, JFK, Got Milk). Tap Water is tighter (64% of presenter time at 30 to 45% of frame height). The kitchen video (Groceries) uses a wide standing shot at the counter: face under 18% of frame height for 51% of presenter time, props on the counter.
- **B-cam:** a side or profile angle for 10 to 18% of presenter time in the 2025 videos and JFK (2023): JFK in profile against a wall map (P01), Groceries side close (P03). McDonald's (2021) and Got Milk (2023) have almost none (0 to 2%). In his interview he specs a warm, cosy look with an A-cam and a B-cam.
- **Jump cuts change size:** 45 to 88% of cuts from his face to his face change framing by more than 15%; punch-ins median 1.31 to 1.56x, pull-backs about as often.
- **Extreme close-up for the sponsor read** (face 50 to 66% of frame height, McDonald's 7:07 to 8:37, P06).
- **Look:** dark, low-key sets lit by warm practical lamps with deep falloff (JFK yellow flowers and a lamp, Tap Water bottles in bokeh, USA 1.0 desk lamps). Measured on 150 frames per video: his frames average **32 to 79 luma out of 255** (1955 is the exception at 140, a bright composite), warm (red minus blue +3 to +18); inserts are bright paper at 90 to 148. ## 14. Method, checks and limits

- **Downloads:** six videos (two at 1080p for text detail, four at 720p), two behind-the-scenes videos at 360p and three research audio files; 1.34 GB in total; logged with titles, URLs and sizes in `/Volumes/T7/OB-EDITS/references/johnny-harris-2026-10-23/DOWNLOADS.md`. Captions were mostly blocked (HTTP 429), so every video was transcribed locally with whisper.cpp base.en; YouTube word timings exist for Groceries and Tap Water only.
- **Cuts:** every frame reduced to 64x36 greyscale; a cut is a frame whose best motion-aligned correlation with the previous frame drops below 0.5, or below 0.8 with no similar change within 6 frames either side (which rejects animation on twos, walking cut-outs and parallax), at least 3 frames after the last cut. Tuned by eye on before/after sheets from 1955 (36 labelled pairs) and checked on sheets of detected cuts and near-misses from McDonald's and Tap Water. Against the 10-20 hand-checked Got Milk list (975 cuts) it finds 946; within 90 ms, 87% of its cuts are on the list and 89% of the list is found. ffmpeg's `scene` score was tried first and dropped: on 1955 its count ran from 117 to 1,297 between thresholds 0.5 and 0.08, and a sampled 0.15 to 0.20 band was almost all film flicker and stepped animation inside composited scenes. It undercounts in-place swaps on a locked shot (Groceries' chart-and-prop changes overhead) and changes inside composites.
- **His face:** Apple Vision face boxes on a frame every 0.5 s, a Vision feature print of each face crop, k-means into 14 clusters per video, every cluster labelled by eye from 8-frame sheets (mixed clusters split again), then spot checks of random no-face frames. Got Milk check: 53% here against 54% counted by eye in the 10-20 study. Presenter layouts (composited or not) clustered on whole-frame feature prints and labelled by eye. Profile share from Vision yaw; framing from face-box height.
- **Voices:** a Gaussian model of Johnny's voice (MFCC means and spreads over 0.5 s windows taken while he is on screen and talking). As a check, windows with an interviewee on screen were recognised as another voice 24 to 91% of the time (McDonald's lowest, Groceries highest), so other voices are missed in places and the own-sound clip counts are a floor.
- **Music:** his voice is mono and the score is stereo, so the side channel carries the bed; K-weighted levels on 400 ms blocks; the bed's centre level estimated from pure-music moments. USA 1.0 is excluded because its voice leaks into the side channel (envelope correlation 0.63), so none of its music numbers are valid. About 4 dB of uncertainty on bed levels.
- **Sound effects:** inferred from spectrograms and transient counts, not listened to.
- **Most replayed:** YouTube's heatmap from each video's info JSON (100 bins of 14 to 20 s), peaks after 0:30, with the transcript and frames at each.
- **Insert categories:** 288 inserts sampled evenly (48 per video), labelled by eye from mid-frames: `an_combined/insert_sample_labels.json`.
- **Per-video data:** `*/an/pace.json` (every shot with its label), `voices.json`, `music.json`, `anchor.json`, `summary.json` (per-minute curves, chapters, heatmap); combined in `an_combined/metrics.json`. The study folder takes about 10 GB on the T7: exFAT stores each of the ~24,000 small frame JPEGs (and each macOS `._` file) in a 128 KB block. Most of it is regenerable intermediates in each `*/an/` folder (`f05/` frames, the 64x36 dumps `thumbs64.rgb` at 1.8 GB, `audio16k.wav`); deleting them is OB's call, nothing reads them after this study.
